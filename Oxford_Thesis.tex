\chapter*{Abstract}
\thispagestyle{empty}

Your abstract goes here. Check your departmental regulations, but this is
usually expected to be under 300 words --- roughly one page.

A workable structure is one paragraph per move: (1) context and why the
problem matters, (2) the gap, (3) what you did, (4) what you found, and
(5) what it means more broadly.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

\chapter{Introduction}
\label{ch:intro}

Open with two or three paragraphs that set the scene: what the general
problem is, why it matters, and the specific gap in current understanding
that this thesis addresses. Cite the key prior work here rather than
deferring all of it to Chapter~\ref{ch:background} \cite{kenny_transthoracic_1992}.


\section{Motivation}
\label{sec:motivation}

Explain in more depth why the gap identified above is worth closing.
A useful pattern is to close this section by stating the motivation as a
short numbered or lettered list: (i) \ldots, (ii) \ldots, (iii) \ldots


\section{Contributions}
\label{sec:contributions}

This thesis makes the following contributions:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{First contribution.} One or two sentences on what it is
    and what it establishes.

  \item \textbf{Second contribution.} \ldots

  \item \textbf{Third contribution.} \ldots
\end{enumerate}

If you have a single figure that summarises the whole pipeline or result,
this is the place for it.

\section{Thesis Overview}
\label{sec:overview}

The thesis is structured as follows:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Chapter~\ref{ch:background} -- Background:} the foundations
    needed to follow the rest of the thesis.

  \item \textbf{Chapter~\ref{ch:related} -- Related Work:} prior literature,
    and where this work sits within it.

  \item \textbf{Chapter~\ref{ch:method} -- Method:} the methodology developed
    in this thesis.

  \item \textbf{Chapter~\ref{ch:experiments} -- Experiments and Results:} the
    empirical evaluation.

  \item \textbf{Chapter~\ref{ch:conclusion} -- Conclusion:} findings,
    limitations, and future directions.
\end{itemize}
